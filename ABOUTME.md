## Why I Started BIZZ PROJECT

BIZZ PROJECT started from a vision I kept developing while learning
cybersecurity.

I became interested not only in detecting security threats, but in how
different parts of a SOC could work together:

**MONITOR → DETECT → ANALYZE → PROTECT**

I started imagining a system that could collect security evidence,
detect suspicious behavior, correlate events, help investigate
incidents, assist threat hunting, and eventually recommend defensive
actions.

I also became interested in how AI could assist a security analyst
without replacing evidence, human judgment, or accountability.

That became BIZZ PROJECT.

But BIZZ PROJECT has another purpose.

It is also my learning environment.

Instead of only reading about cybersecurity, I want to progressively
build what I am learning.

## AI Does Not Mean Automatic Truth

One of the principles I want BIZZ PROJECT to follow is:

> **AI reasons about evidence. AI does not create the evidence.**

I apply a similar principle to my own learning.

Just because an AI gives me an architecture, explanation, policy, or
piece of code does not mean I should automatically consider it correct.

I want to learn to ask:

- Why?
- What evidence supports this?
- What assumption are we making?
- What could go wrong?
- How can we test it?
- Is there a simpler solution?
- Do I actually understand this?

If I cannot explain something yet, then it represents something I still
need to learn.

## My Learning Rule

The development process I want to follow is:

**LEARN → OBSERVE → INVESTIGATE MANUALLY → BUILD WITH AI → READ → TEST → BREAK SAFELY → FIX → EXPLAIN → DOCUMENT**

I do not want BIZZ PROJECT to become a large codebase that I cannot
explain.

Before automating important cybersecurity concepts, I want to understand
them at least conceptually.

When we build a failed-authentication detection, for example, I want to
first understand the events myself.

When we eventually investigate suspicious PowerShell activity, I want to
understand why the behavior may be suspicious.

When AI eventually analyzes incidents, I want to understand how its
conclusions relate to the underlying evidence.

The goal is not only:

> **Make BIZZ PROJECT smarter.**

The goal is:

> **As BIZZ PROJECT becomes more capable, I become more capable too.**

---

## Evidence Before Conclusions

One of my favorite principles in this project is:

> **Evidence Before Conclusions.**

I want BIZZ PROJECT to distinguish between:

**FACT** — directly supported by evidence.

**INFERENCE** — reasonably derived from evidence.

**HYPOTHESIS** — something that may be true but requires investigation.

**UNKNOWN** — something the available evidence cannot establish.

I want to apply the same thinking to myself.

I do not want to pretend I know something that I do not know.

It is okay for me to say:

> "I don't understand this yet."

Then I study it.

Then I test it.

Then eventually I should be able to explain it myself.
